The Insider Guide to Touring the Greenbrier Bunker: Can You Go Alone?

Built in the 1950s with the code name "Project Greek Island," it served as a top-secret relocation facility for the United States Congress. Hidden beneath a newly constructed wing of the hotel, the facility was designed to house all 535 members of Congress and their essential support staff (about 1,100 people) in the event of nuclear war. Construction began in 1958 and finished in 1961. To hide the massive project, the government told the public they were just building a new, air-conditioned addition to the luxury resort. It was fortified with a 25-ton blast door, decontamination chambers, and independent water and power supplies. The bunker was stocked with enough food and water to sustain everyone for 60 days.

Inside, the shelter featured separate auditoriums for the House of Representatives and the Senate, an intensive care unit, a clinic, a pharmacy, and a pathological waste incinerator that could serve as a crematorium. For 30 years, it was maintained in a state of constant readiness by undercover government employees who posed as hotel audio-visual or repair staff. The secret was kept until an investigative article by reporter Ted Gup exposed the bunker's existence in The Washington Post in 1992. Following its exposure, the bunker was quickly decommissioned by the government. Today, it is open to the public for guided tours, providing a fascinating look into a major piece of Cold War history.

Do You Need a Group to Tour the Greenbrier Bunker?

Clear answer first: No, individuals do not need to bring their own group to tour the bunker. Solo travelers, couples, and small families can easily purchase individual tickets and join a pre-scheduled public group tour.

Essential Bunker Tour Rules to Know Before You Go:

  • Reservations are mandatory: You cannot just show up and walk in; you must book a time slot in advance.
  • Age Limits: The minimum age requirement is 10 years old. Children under 10 are strictly not permitted on the 90-minute tour.
  • No Electronics: Cameras, cell phones, or electronic equipment are allowed. All electronics must be surrendered at check-in.

Exploring West Virginia: Beyond the Bunker

There is so much to do in the area! You can play golf at the nearby Greenbrier Golf Club or take a drive to visit the famous Mothman Museum in Point Pleasant. With so many diverse attractions, this region makes a wonderful weekend getaway or an extended trip exploring the natural beauty of West Virginia.
