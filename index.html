Built in the 1950s with the code name "Project Greek Island," the Greenbrier Bunker served as a top-secret relocation facility for the United States Congress. Hidden beneath a newly constructed wing of the hotel, the facility was designed to house all 535 members of Congress and their essential support staff (about 1,100 people) in the event of nuclear war.

Construction began in 1958 and finished in 1961. To hide the massive project, the government told the public they were just building a new, air-conditioned addition to the luxury resort. It was fortified with a 25-ton blast door, decontamination chambers, and independent water and power supplies. The bunker was stocked with enough food and water to sustain everyone for 60 days.

Inside, the shelter featured separate auditoriums for the House of Representatives and the Senate, an intensive care unit, a clinic, a pharmacy, and a pathological waste incinerator. For 30 years, it was maintained in a state of constant readiness by undercover government employees who posed as hotel audio-visual or repair staff.

The secret was kept until an investigative article by reporter Ted Gup exposed the bunker’s existence in The Washington Post in 1992. Following its exposure, the bunker was quickly decommissioned by the government. Today, it is open to the public for guided tours, providing a fascinating look into a major piece of Cold War history.

Do You Need a Group to Tour the Greenbrier Bunker?

Clear answer first: No, individuals do not need to bring their own group to tour the bunker. Solo travelers, couples, and small families can easily purchase individual tickets and join a pre-scheduled public group tour.

Essential Bunker Tour Rules to Know Before You Go:

  • Reservations are mandatory: You cannot just show up and walk in; you must book a time slot in advance through the official Greenbrier Bunker Tours portal .
  • Age Limits: The minimum age requirement is 10 years old. Children under 10 are strictly not permitted on the 90-minute tour.
  • Strict No-Electronics Policy: Cameras, cell phones, smartwatches, or recording equipment are completely forbidden. All electronic items and large bags must be left in your vehicle or surrendered to secure lockers at check-in.

Exploring West Virginia: High-End Land Packages & Beyond

The bunker is just the beginning of what this incredible region has to offer. The Greenbrier itself spans 11,000 pristine acres in White Sulphur Springs, boasting five championship golf courses, a world-class mineral spa, and over 50 daily outdoor activities ranging from falconry to fly fishing. It represents the pinnacle of luxury historic American resorts and makes the perfect anchor for an upscale weekend getaway.

If you love regional roadside history and want to expand your West Virginia road trip, consider pairing your luxury resort stay with a scenic mountain drive. You can explore the historic nearby town of Lewisburg or embark on a multi-day itinerary to experience the outdoor beauty of the New River Gorge.
