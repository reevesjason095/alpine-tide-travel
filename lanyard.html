The Green Lanyard That Secures Calmer Airport Travel

A simple guide to airport support, the Hidden Disabilities Sunflower Program, and TSA Cares.

Traveling through an airport with sensory processing sensitivities or autism can feel incredibly overwhelming. Fortunately, there is a simple visual tool designed to quietly signal airport staff that you or your child might need a little extra grace.

What is the green lanyard with sunflowers?

Clear answer first: It is the official symbol of the Hidden Disabilities Sunflower Program, a free initiative that discreetly signals to trained airport staff, flight crews, and TSA agents that a traveler has an invisible disability and may require extra time, patience, or clearer communication.

Essential Lanyard Rules to Know Before You Go

  • It is completely free: You never have to pay for a lanyard, provide medical documentation, or disclose a specific diagnosis to receive one.
  • It is not a "fast pass": The lanyard does not allow you to skip security queues or bypass standard TSA regulations. It simply changes how staff interact with and guide you through those spaces.
  • Where to pick them up: You can collect them at airport information desks, guest services, or ticketing counters.
  • Get it mailed early: Major international hubs like New York's JFK and LaGuardia may mail a lanyard to your home for free if you request one online in advance.

The Secret Weapon to Pair With It: TSA Cares

To maximize airport support, always combine the Sunflower Lanyard with the free federal TSA Cares program.

By calling the TSA Cares helpline at (855) 787-2227 at least 72 hours before your flight, TSA can assign a dedicated Passenger Support Specialist to help guide your family through screening.
